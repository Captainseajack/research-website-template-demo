> **Template example:** Replace this material with the complete research article for your topic.

## The question

State the precise question being investigated. Define any disputed terms and explain the scope of the inquiry so readers know what the article does and does not attempt to establish.

## The short answer

Give the conclusion in direct language. A reader should not have to wait until the end of the article to learn what the evidence appears to show.

## What the record establishes

### Finding one

Explain the strongest finding and link directly to the relevant items in the [source record]({{ '/sources/' | relative_url }}). Distinguish what a document says from what you infer from it.

### Finding two

Add the next major finding. Use dates, quotations, page numbers, and statistical context where they help the reader verify the claim.

### Finding three

Explain how the evidence fits together. Identify whether multiple sources independently confirm a point or repeat the same underlying account.

## Context and chronology

Provide the history needed to understand the issue. A timeline, table, or clearly ordered sequence can be used when chronology is central to the argument.

## Competing explanations

Describe the strongest counterargument fairly. Explain which evidence supports it, which evidence weighs against it, and what remains unresolved.

## Limitations

- Identify information that is unavailable or incomplete.
- Explain important source limitations.
- State what new evidence could change the assessment.

## Conclusion

Return to the main answer and explain why it follows from the evidence. Readers can inspect the [complete source list]({{ '/sources/' | relative_url }}) or see [material added after publication]({{ '/updates/' | relative_url }}).
